# Data governance and responsible AI note

**Project:** Enrolment Hub (see `README.md`). **Scope:** what data this app holds and where it came from, what handling real student data would require, how Indigenous data sovereignty applies, and how the AI features are kept honest.
**Voice:** sections 1, 2, 4 and 5 are written as a technical record. Section 3 gives the frameworks and where they bite in this project; **section 3.3 is mine to write in my own words** and is left open.

## 1. What data this app holds, and why

| Data | What it is | Where it came from | Who controls it | Status in the app |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Students and their results, plans and timetables | invented people and invented histories | generated by the project's own seed and demo presets | the project | **synthetic only**; the footer of every page says so |
| Degree, major, sub-major and subject structure (codes, names, credit points, options) | facts about one degree | the UTS Handbook (2027 pages), supplied by the student by pasting each page | UTS | checked against the supplied pages on 8 November 2026; `features/subject_catalogue/handbook_2027.py` keeps that copy as test data |
| Subject and program descriptions | short summaries | written in the project's own words from the supplied pages, with the source and date shown | UTS holds the originals | paraphrased, never copied; every page links to the official text |
| Assessment tasks and weightings | by category and weight | the 2027 Handbook pages (transdisciplinary electives) or earlier supplied data, each marked with its source and year | UTS | labelled "indicative"; one subject's page lists none and the app says exactly that |
| Session availability | which session a subject runs in | the 2027 Handbook for the eight transdisciplinary electives only | UTS | every other subject's sessions are **illustrative** and say so |
| Sample electives | twelve invented subjects (`SYN101` to `SYN112`) | invented here | the project | labelled as invented; they have no Handbook link |

**Why synthetic only.** A learning project does not need real people's records, and holding them would create duties (security, retention, consent) that a prototype cannot meet. Every account that exists is either the demo student or a student created for development.

### Decisions about the Handbook data

- **The Handbook was not scraped.** The site tells automated tools to stay out, and a program that goes around that would be taking data without permission. All Handbook content in this app was supplied by a person (copied from the pages and pasted), is attributed, is dated, and is linked back to the official page. This is slower and is the correct trade.
- **Facts are used; text is not copied.** Codes, names, credit points and weightings are facts about a degree. Descriptive prose is paraphrased and a test checks that no two summaries share a run of six words, so a summary cannot be a pasted original.
- **Nothing is presented as more official than it is.** Pages say "indicative", "illustrative" or "not yet populated". There are no UTS logos or colours, and the README says this is an independent learning project, not a UTS system.
- **A link may only point at the Handbook.** A database check refuses any other address in a stored Handbook link, so a "Handbook" link cannot be turned into a link elsewhere.

## 2. Handling real student data: what would be required

This is a list of what a real deployment would have to settle first. It is not legal advice, and the university's privacy office, not this project, decides these.

1. **Purpose and minimisation.** Collect only what each feature needs. The planner needs results and a plan; it does not need a date of birth or a home address.
2. **Legal basis and notice.** Privacy law applies to student records (the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and, for a NSW public university, NSW privacy legislation), including telling students what is collected and why, and the duty to report eligible data breaches.
3. **Access control and audit.** The current design (a student sees only their own record) is a starting point. Staff access would need roles, logging of who looked at what, and review.
4. **Storage and transport.** TLS, encryption at rest, backups, a retention and deletion schedule, and a production server. `docs/SECURITY.md` lists which of these the prototype does not have.
5. **AI and third parties.** By default questions go to a model on the same computer and never leave it. A cloud model would need an agreement, a privacy assessment and a decision on whether student data may be sent at all.
6. **Authority for the data.** The records belong to the university and to the students they describe. A tool built on them needs the university's approval, not just a technical fix.

## 3. Indigenous data sovereignty, FPIC, the AIATSIS Code and CARE

### 3.1 What the frameworks ask (in brief)

- **Indigenous data sovereignty** is the right of Indigenous peoples to govern the collection, ownership and use of data about their peoples, lands and cultures.
- **The CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance** (Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics) are a people-and-purpose-oriented complement to the FAIR data principles (Carroll et al., 2020).
- **Free, prior and informed consent (FPIC)** comes from the UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples: consent that is given freely, before a project, with enough information, and that can be refused.
- **The AIATSIS Code of Ethics for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Research** (2020) rests on four principles: Indigenous self-determination, Indigenous leadership, impact and value, and sustainability and accountability.

### 3.2 Where they apply to this project (specific, not general)

1. **The app holds no Indigenous data.** No student record carries any cultural or identity field, because the students are invented. This is the right current position, and also the safe default: **a real enrolment system is exactly where Indigenous status is routinely recorded (admissions, equity reporting), and adding such a field would turn this tool into a holder of Indigenous data.** If that ever happened, Authority to control and FPIC would apply before the first record was stored: who decides what the data is used for, who can see it, whether it can be used to train or tune an AI, and how a person can withdraw.
2. **The catalogue describes subjects that concern Indigenous knowledge and data.** Two descriptions in the app touch it: 31265 (a design challenge with an Indigenous Australian context) and 57304 (the ethics of data and AI, including Indigenous data sovereignty). A short paraphrase of such a subject can misrepresent it. The protections today are that each description is kept neutral and short and every subject page links to the official text. **Still to do:** compare both descriptions against the official 2027 text and, in a real deployment, have the subject coordinator (and where appropriate Indigenous staff) approve any description of Indigenous content instead of paraphrasing it.
3. **AI answers can misrepresent.** The explanations come from a language model whose training data's provenance includes material about Indigenous peoples that the project cannot inspect. The design limits the harm: the model is asked to explain facts the code already computed and cannot add new ones (`docs/SECURITY.md`, the shared answer checks), it is off by default, and it is not asked to produce cultural content. That does not make the model's training data governed in the CARE sense. It makes the *use* narrow.
4. **Impact and value.** The AIATSIS principle of impact and value asks who benefits. Here the beneficiary is the student building the tool. That is acceptable for a project with no Indigenous data, and it is a reason not to claim more.
5. **FPIC does not apply to this project as built, because no Indigenous community's data or knowledge is used.** It would apply the moment that changed, and the answer to "may we?" would have to be asked before building, not after.

## 4. AI features: grounding, citations, insufficient context and human review

| Practice | What the app does | Where | Checked by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Deterministic code decides; the model explains** | Eligibility, graduation date, workload and "good to switch" are computed by tested code. The model is given those results and may only put them into words | `features/*/service.py`, `ai_services` | the checks below, and the rules' own tests |
| **Grounding and citations** | Answers are built only from retrieved sources (the app's own rules and catalogue) and show their sources | `ai_services/rag` | `tests/ai_services/rag/` |
| **Saying "I can't tell"** | If nothing relevant is found, the app says so and does not call the model | `ai_services/rag` | `tests/ai_services/rag/test_answer_and_model.py::test_a_long_injection_in_the_question_finds_no_sources_and_never_reaches_the_model` |
| **No invented facts** | Every number, link, quoted instruction or claim of an action in an answer is checked against what was provided; a failed check replaces the answer with a plain one | `ai_services/checks.py` | `tests/ai_services/test_checks.py` |
| **Human in the loop** | The tools only look things up; nothing changes unless the student presses the button, and the swap page shows the cost first | `ai_services/tools` | `tests/ai_services/ai_mode/test_agent.py::test_running_the_agent_never_changes_any_data` |
| **Opt-in and private** | AI mode is off by default, and the default model runs on the same computer | `ai_services/llm.py` | `tests/ai_services/web/test_ai_mode_pages.py::test_ai_mode_is_off_by_default_and_the_box_is_hidden` |
| **Honest labels** | "App estimate, not a UTS rating", "illustrative", "indicative", "not yet populated" | templates | `tests/features/subject_catalogue/test_td_assessments.py` and others |

**Limits.** The checks stop invented numbers and leaked instructions; they cannot guarantee that an explanation is wise or fair. There is no measured evaluation set yet. A student could still over-trust a confident answer, which is why the app shows its sources and why the verdicts come from code.

## 5. What I would change next

1. **Before any real data:** a privacy impact assessment with the university's privacy office, and a decision on retention and roles.
2. **Before any equity or identity field:** consult the university's Indigenous governance and leadership (for UTS, the Jumbunna Institute), and apply the CARE principles and FPIC to the design, not just the policy.
3. **Check the two descriptions** that touch Indigenous content against the official text, and prefer a link over a paraphrase for anything cultural.
4. **An AI evaluation set** (known questions, expected sources, refusals and attack prompts) run in CI, so claims about the AI are measured.
5. **Ask UTS about an official data feed** for the Handbook, so the data no longer depends on pasting pages.
