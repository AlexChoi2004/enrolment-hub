Help and demo guide

This is a demonstration. Everything about the student, their results, sessions, class times and due weeks is simulated. Nothing here is a genuine UTS academic record. Subject details and prerequisites come from the public UTS Handbook, which is itself indicative.

On this page: Quick start Starting points Things to test AI mode Worth showing off What each tab does Questions

Quick start

  1. Log in as the simulated student: john.doe@student.uni.edu.au with the password Johndoe#1. There is no public sign-up, because at a real university your login comes from admissions.
  2. John starts as a first-year student in Year 1, Semester 1 (Autumn 2027), with no major and no sub-major chosen yet, like a real first-year. Choose a major (48 credit points), then a sub-major or general electives (24 credit points). You can enrol in core subjects straight away.
  3. To reach a later situation quickly, press Reset in the bar under the header and pick a starting point (below).

Starting points

Press Reset in the bar under the header, choose one, tick the box and confirm. It replaces the demo student's simulated records, so you never have to pass semester after semester to test something late in a degree.

Failed subjects and rollbacks

A failed prerequisite, then a retake Start from: {{ presets["failed"].title }}
  1. Open the Degree planner. Programming 2 is under Failed subjects with an Add retake to plan button, and Data Structures and Algorithms is flagged: "Needs 48024 Programming 2 (failed: retake it first)".
  2. Open Subjects and enrol in Programming 2 for Autumn 2027. There is room, because only 18 of 24 credit points are in use.
  3. Press Complete semester, leave every subject on Passed, press Review, then Confirm and save.
  4. Open Results.

You should see: Programming 2 twice, Failed in Spring 2026 and Passed in Autumn 2027, with its 6 credit points counted once. Data Structures and Algorithms is no longer blocked. Press Roll back a semester to undo it all and try again.

Fail a subject and watch the plan react Start from: {{ presets["fresh"].title }}
  1. Open Subjects and enrol in Programming 1, Mathematics 1, Communication for IT Professionals and Discrete Mathematics for Autumn 2027.
  2. In the Degree planner, find Programming 2 (it needs Programming 1) and add it to Spring 2027.
  3. Press Complete semester. Set Programming 1 to Failed, leave the others on Passed, press Review, then Confirm and save.

You should see: you are now in Year 1, Semester 2 (Spring 2027). Results shows Programming 1 as Failed (FL) and the other three as Passed. The planner lists Programming 1 under "Failed subjects", and Programming 2 is still planned but flagged "Needs 41039 Programming 1 (failed: retake it first)". In Subjects, Programming 1 has a "Failed: retake needed" badge and enrolling in Programming 2 is refused.

Finish a semester without making up results Start from: {{ presets["fresh"].title }}
  1. Enrol in a subject, then press Complete semester.
  2. Leave every subject on No result yet, review and confirm.

You should see: the session moves on, but Results is unchanged: no pass and no fail was invented, and the subject is an outstanding requirement again.

Undo a semester Start from: any
  1. Press Complete semester in the bar under the header and finish a semester with some results.
  2. Press Roll back a semester, read what will be undone, and confirm.

You should see: you are back in the earlier session, the results it recorded are gone, and the subjects are enrolled again with their class choices and plan entries as they were. You can roll back as many completed semesters as you have finished, one at a time.

Changing major, sub-major or electives

A swap that costs time Start from: {{ presets["sample"].title }}
  1. Open Subjects, press Swap major, and open Artificial Intelligence and Data Analytics.
  2. Read the Swap advice box, then try pressing the swap button without ticking the box.

You should see: "Possible, but it costs time". Credit points would stop counting, graduation moves from Spring 2028 to Autumn 2029, and the swap is refused until you tick that you understand the extra subjects and delay. Nothing is deleted from your record either way.

A risky swap needs your confirmation Start from: {{ presets["late"].title }}
  1. Open Subjects, press Swap major, and open Networking and Cybersecurity.
  2. Read the advice, press the swap without the tick (it is refused), then tick the box and confirm.

You should see: "Think carefully", because you are 88% through your current major and a lot would stop counting. The projected graduation session moves later. The tick is the only way the swap goes ahead: the decision is always yours.

Keep a sub-major's subjects as general electives Start from: {{ presets["sample"].title }}
  1. Open Subjects, press Swap sub-major, then choose General electives.
  2. Find the box Count these as general electives? and tick all four subjects.
  3. Confirm the swap.

You should see: with all four ticked nothing is lost, so the swap is "Good to switch" and needs no cost tick. Afterwards the Subjects page shows "Counted 4 subject(s) as general electives" and General electives with 4 of 4 chosen. Try again ticking only two, and the cost tick comes back.

AI mode

AI mode is off until you turn it on (the switch is in the top right). With it on, the Degree planner, Timetable and the major, sub-major and electives pages have a question box. The model runs on this computer, so your questions don't leave it. It can be wrong, so check what it shows you.

  • Rules and subjects: answered from the app's own sources. If they don't cover it you get "Not enough context", not a guess.
  • About you ("Why is my Autumn heavy?", "Can I enrol in Programming 2?"): the AI looks things up in your own records with read-only tools and shows you what it looked at. It can only look things up: it can never enrol, withdraw, plan, move or swap anything.
  • Swaps: Explain this swap runs three roles: an Analyst (code) gets the facts, an Advisor (the AI) explains them, and a Checker (code) rejects an explanation that doesn't match. The decision always stays with you.

  4. Ask What is the capital of France? You should get "Not enough context".

Worth showing off

  • The code decides and the AI explains. Prerequisites, workload, assessment load and swap verdicts are fixed rules you can read and test.
  • You always decide. A risky swap needs your tick, checked on the server, and the AI can only look things up.
  • Every AI answer is checked in code (real citations, numbers only from the sources, no links, no claims of action), and if a check fails you see the facts instead.
  • "Not enough context" instead of guessing, and unconfirmed data is labelled as indicative, illustrative or unverified.
  • Failed subjects are handled properly: every attempt is kept, credit points count once, and a failed prerequisite blocks what depends on it, with the reason.
  • Swaps are never blocked, but you see what would stop counting first, and can keep subjects as electives when there is room.
  • Roll back a semester and starting points make it quick to test anything.
  • A read-only MCP server offers the same tools to another AI app on a snapshot it cannot write back (see docs/MCP.md in the project).

What each tab does

Subjects
Every subject in your degree with whether you can enrol, and why or why not. Failed subjects show "Failed: retake needed".
Degree planner
Plans future sessions. It shows completed, in progress, planned, failed and blocked subjects, and a workload verdict for each session.
Timetable
Your classes for a session. Clashes are allowed but drawn in red.
Record
Your degree progress, oldest session first.
Results
Every attempt with mark, grade and outcome, newest session first (you can switch the order).
Simulation bar
On every page, under the header: Complete semester (choose Passed, Failed or No result yet for each subject), Roll back a semester (undo the last one) and Reset (pick a starting point). Only the simulated student has it.

Questions

Is this a real UTS system?
No. It is an independent student prototype using simulated students and results.
Why can't I create an account?
Public sign-up is off: real students are given their login by the university. One simulated student keeps the demo simple and lets you test any scenario.
How do marks become grades?
HD is 85 or more, D 75 to 84, C 65 to 74, P 50 to 64, and FL is below 50. If you leave the mark blank, a plausible one is filled in.
What does "No result yet" do?
It records nothing for that subject (no pass, no fail), ends the enrolment, and the subject goes back to your outstanding requirements.
A planned subject vanished. Where did it go?
Nothing is deleted. If its session has passed it appears under "Needs rescheduling", and if it depends on a failed subject it is flagged with the reason.
Are credit points ever counted twice?
No. A subject can be passed only once, so its credit points count once however many attempts it took.
How do I start again, or jump to a later point?
Press Reset in the bar under the header, then choose a starting point.
Where did the Simulator tab go?
Its buttons are in the bar under the header of every page, so you never leave what you are looking at.
Why did the AI show sources or facts instead of an answer?
Every written answer is checked first. If a check fails (for example it used a number that isn't in the sources) the AI's text is dropped and you see what it found instead.
