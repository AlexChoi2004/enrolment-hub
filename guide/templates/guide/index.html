Help and demo guide

This is a demonstration. Everything about the student, their results, sessions, class times and due weeks is simulated. Nothing here is a genuine UTS academic record. Subject details and prerequisites come from the public UTS Handbook, which is itself indicative.

Getting started

  1. Log in as the simulated student: john.doe@student.uni.edu.au with the password Johndoe#1. There is no public sign-up, because at a real university your login comes from admissions.
  2. John starts as a first-year student in Year 1, Semester 1 (Autumn 2027), with no major and no sub-major chosen yet, like a real first-year. The Subjects page prompts you: choose a major (48 credit points), then a sub-major or general electives (24 credit points). You can enrol in core subjects straight away.
  3. Use Subjects to enrol, Degree planner to plan future sessions, Timetable to pick classes, Results to see grades, and Simulator to finish a semester.

What each tab does

Subjects
Every subject in your degree with whether you can enrol, and why or why not. Failed subjects show "Failed: retake needed".
Degree planner
Plans future sessions. It shows completed, in progress, planned, failed and blocked subjects, and a workload verdict for each session.
Timetable
Your classes for a session. Clashes are allowed but drawn in red.
Record
Your degree progress, oldest session first.
Results
Every attempt with mark, grade and outcome, newest session first (you can switch the order).
Simulator
Finish a semester by choosing Passed, Failed or No result yet for each subject. Only the simulated student has this.

Try these scenarios

Start each one from a fresh student (the guide assumes you have not chosen a major yet): Simulator, then Reset the simulation, choose Start as a first-year student, tick the box and press Reset.

Try this: fail a prerequisite subject

  1. Open Subjects and enrol in Programming 1, Mathematics 1, Communication for IT Professionals and Discrete Mathematics for Autumn 2027.
  2. Open the Degree planner, find Programming 2 (it needs Programming 1) and add it to Spring 2027.
  3. In the Degree planner press Complete semester. Set Programming 1 to Failed and leave the others on Passed. Press Review, then Confirm and save.

What you should see: you are now in Year 1, Semester 2 (Spring 2027). Results shows Programming 1 as Failed (FL) and the other three as Passed. The Degree planner lists Programming 1 under "Failed subjects" with an Add retake to plan button, and Programming 2 is still planned but flagged with the reason "Needs 41039 Programming 1 (failed: retake it first)". In Subjects, Programming 1 has a "Failed: retake needed" badge, Programming 2 says Can't enrol, and trying to enrol in Programming 2 is refused.

Try this: retake and pass

  1. Continue from the scenario above. In Subjects, enrol in Programming 1 again (Spring 2027 is the open session).
  2. Open the Timetable and choose a class for it.
  3. Press Complete semester in the Degree planner, set Programming 1 to Passed, review and confirm.

What you should see: Results shows both attempts for Programming 1: Failed (attempt 1) and Passed (attempt 2), and its credit points are counted once. Programming 2 is no longer blocked. It was planned for Spring 2027, which has passed, so the planner lists it under Needs rescheduling: move it to a future session, then enrol when that session is current.

Try this: skip a semester without making up results

  1. Enrol in a subject, then press Skip to next semester.
  2. Leave every subject on No result yet, review and confirm.

What you should see: the session moves on, but Results is unchanged: no pass and no fail was invented. The subject is an outstanding requirement again.

Try this: change major or sub-major

  1. Choose a major and a sub-major first (the Subjects page prompts you), enrol in some subjects and complete a semester or two. Then open Subjects and press Swap major (or Swap sub-major).
  2. Open a different one and read the preview, then confirm. If you have subjects in progress that would stop counting, a tick box offers to withdraw you from them.

What you should see: completed subjects that are in the new major still count, and the rest stay on your Results but are labelled "Doesn't count towards your degree". Nothing is deleted, and the credit points still to do are recalculated. In real life a change of major needs a request to UTS: this app is a planning tool and shows the consequences first.

Try this: explore a student two years in

Simulator, Reset the simulation, choose Load the sample two-year history. You get sixteen completed subjects and four in progress (Year 3, Semester 1), which is a good starting point for exploring blocked subjects and sub-majors.

AI mode

AI mode is off until you turn it on (the switch is in the top right). With it on, the Degree planner, Timetable and the major, sub-major and electives pages have a box to ask about a rule or a subject. Answers come only from the app's own sources (subject details, majors, and how the app works), are checked by code before they are shown, and always list their sources. If the sources don't cover your question you get "Not enough context", not a guess. The model runs on this computer, so your questions don't leave it. It can still be wrong, so check the cited source.

If you ask about yourself ("Why is my Autumn heavy?", "Can I enrol in Programming 2?"), the AI looks things up in your own records with read-only tools and shows you what it looked at. It can only look things up: it can never enrol, withdraw, plan or swap anything. On a major, sub-major or electives page, Explain this swap runs three roles: an Analyst (code) gets the facts, an Advisor (the AI) explains them, and a Checker (code) rejects an explanation that doesn't match. The decision always stays with you.

Questions

Is this a real UTS system?
No. It is an independent student prototype using simulated students and results.
Why can't I create an account?
Public sign-up is off: real students are given their login by the university. One simulated student keeps the demo simple and lets you test any scenario.
How do marks become grades?
HD is 85 or more, D 75 to 84, C 65 to 74, P 50 to 64, and FL is below 50. If you leave the mark blank, a plausible one is filled in.
What does "No result yet" do?
It records nothing for that subject (no pass, no fail), ends the enrolment, and the subject goes back to your outstanding requirements.
A planned subject vanished. Where did it go?
Nothing is deleted. If its session has passed it appears under "Needs rescheduling", and if it depends on a failed subject it is flagged with the reason.
Are credit points ever counted twice?
No. A subject can be passed only once, so its credit points count once however many attempts it took.
How do I start again?
Simulator, then Reset the simulation, then choose a fresh first-year student or the sample history.
